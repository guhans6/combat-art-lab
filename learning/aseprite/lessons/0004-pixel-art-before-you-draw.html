Choose what the player sees.
Then choose your pixels.

Your win: write a small, provisional size plan for one character. You do not need to open Aseprite or Godot yet.

Pixel art uses deliberate pixel placement to suggest shapes. The job is to make your character read clearly at the size people will see it. For your character, tied hair, an olive jacket, wide jeans and a cream tote matter before tiny fabric marks. Artist Derek Yu’s introduction ↗

1 · “Size” can mean four different things

NameWhat it meansYour example
Drawing canvasThe whole image grid, including empty transparent space.Your practice file is 128 × 192 pixels.
Visible character sizeThe space the actual drawing occupies inside that grid.This copy’s drawing occupies a 72 × 183 bounding box. Empty margins are not extra height on the character.
Game design viewThe frame you compose the game around: a base viewport.We will imagine 640 × 360 for the example.
Displayed windowThe output area showing that game view.The example can fill 1920 × 1080 at 3×.

The first two belong to the artwork. Godot connects them to the last two. Zooming in while editing changes only your view; it does not add drawing pixels. Aseprite canvas ↗ · Godot base size ↗

3 · Decide how much of the game view the character fills

Imagine a character drawn 96 pixels tall in a 640 × 360 game view. At 1× sprite scale and 1× camera zoom, it fills about 27% of the view height. Showing the entire view at 3× makes the character 288 display pixels tall; it still fills 27%.

4 · Choose a small test, then draw the important shapes

  1. Describe the view and pick a trial size.Choose a rough character share of the frame, then a drawing grid that might support it. For a new 96-pixel-tall figure at 1×, a 64 × 112 canvas could leave 16 extra rows for margins; its width must also fit the pose. This is one trial, not a size rule. There is no universal “game sprite size”. A square file is not required. Our 128 × 192 file is practice material, not a final answer for your game.
  2. Test identity before decoration.First the outer shape, then clear light/dark masses, then a few grouped colors for hair, jacket, jeans and tote. Finally add the eye, strap or folds that help recognition. Reuse nearby colors while learning. No fixed 16- or 32-color rule is required for this exercise. Outline, shading and final-detail stages ↗
  3. Inspect in context, revise, then animate.Look at the intended game-view size against a representative background. If the face merges, try simplifying its shapes, a different grid or closer framing. For animation, preserve the character’s scale and a consistent ground reference; leave room in the frame for movement.
How does Godot keep the pixels crisp?

For a simple low-resolution pixel-art test, Godot documents viewport stretch mode, integer stretch scale mode, and an aspect policy such as keep. Nearest texture filtering avoids blurred sampling. Integer 2× or 3× enlargement gives even blocks; fractional enlargement can give uneven blocks. Position and camera alignment still matter. These are later setup choices, not prerequisites for today. Does a character have to fit one tile, or use a power-of-two size?

A tile is a repeated environment cell. An ordinary Sprite2D character is a separate texture and can span several cells. Its canvas need not be 32 × 32, square or a power of two. Use dimensions that fit the art, movement and export arrangement; special tile, atlas or platform workflows can add their own constraints. Sprite2D texture and regions ↗

Your two-minute size plan

Write these four lines in a note. Use the example if you want a first hypothesis; you can revise it after seeing a real sprite in Godot.

  1. View: side-view combat; trial design view 640 × 360.
  2. Framing: trial visible character height 96 pixels, about 27% of the view.
  3. Must read: tied hair, open olive jacket, wide jeans, cream tote and connected strap.
  4. First test: keep the existing 128 × 192 practice file for Lessons 1–3; later compare its framing in Godot before redrawing for the proposed 96-pixel target.

These example numbers are provisional. Do not shrink your practice file to 96 pixels just to match the example. Today’s decision is what to test, not a production commitment.

Ask the agent: “Here is my size plan. I want the character to feel ____. Does this framing give enough room?” Explain what changed when you tried the two controls; that gives us something to build the next lesson on.
