# Research: South Indian cues for the stealth/direct-combat fighter

Last researched: **1 November 2026**. This is a bounded reference study for the
working `stealth-fighter` brief. It is not concept approval, a costume reconstruction,
or authorization to generate or construct art.

## Question and evidence boundary

The requested direction is a visible-faced, lean male fighter in his early to
mid-20s, with simple cloth, a compact one-handed sword, and subtle future
construction—not cyberpunk or magical. The user prefers South Indian influence.

The records below are primary collection records from The Metropolitan Museum of Art
(The Met), inspected on the date above. They establish attributes of individual
arms/armor objects, their catalogued culture/geography, material, date, and—in the
linked cases—the depicted object appearance. They **do not** establish a universal
South Indian warrior uniform, a civilian garment, ordinary soldier's equipment, a
particular fighting technique, or the exact look of a historical person. No film,
game, Pinterest, generative-image, or unverified retailer material was used.

The evidence is strongest for a *localized arm-defense silhouette* and a *South
Indian straight-sword precedent*. It is weaker for dress: this short study did not
find a suitable primary record for everyday/ordinary male martial clothing. Temple
sculpture and court/religious imagery were deliberately not used as proof of ordinary
warrior equipment.

### Visual-reference set

Use the three direct images above only as study references; do not import or trace
them into a game asset. The Met marks each linked image Public Domain, but that does
not turn the historical design into an approved game costume. The khanda and wootz
records explicitly have no collection image, so they support catalogued facts rather
than a visual reconstruction.

## Recommended original direction — **Tamil-region arm guard + compact straight sword**

This is a **fictional adaptation**, not a recovered historical kit:

- Start with a lean, athletic adult silhouette; face and short-to-medium practical hair
  stay fully visible. Use a simple fitted, collarless cloth overshirt with a wrapped
  lower panel/short split tabard, narrow trousers, and low boots. This keeps the
  requested cloth-first, mobile read, but its cut is original because this study does
  not establish a period South Indian combat garment.
- Put *one* dark, matte, tapered forearm guard on the sword arm. Borrow only the
  17th-century Thanjavur object's long almond/central-channel silhouette; reduce it
  to two or three large planes, no dense engraving, studs, gold, or mirrored pair.
  Its clean attachment can be a narrow cloth/leather cuff rather than a bulky
  cybernetic gauntlet.
- Make the future cue constructional: clean seam routing, small flush fastening tabs,
  durable laminated cloth, and a deliberately manufactured scabbard throat. Avoid
  LEDs, exposed circuitry, glowing seams, chrome, synthetic bodysuits, or powered
  armor. These details are original design choices, not historical claims.
- Use a **fictional compact one-handed straight sword**: about 55–65 cm blade in the
  finished concept, with a modest broadening toward the tip and a plain, slightly
  flared guard. This compresses the South Indian khanda's broad/straight visual idea
  to fit the requested close-range fighter; the museum blade is 82 cm and should not
  be represented as a compact weapon. A dark horn/wood-like grip and muted steel
  separate material zones without ornamental overload.
- Keep palette/material hierarchy simple: one dark iron, one weathered warm cloth,
  one muted secondary cloth, natural skin/hair, and a small worn-silver edge. The
  arm guard should read as protection first, while cloth/hair/face preserve the
  human, non-cyberpunk read.

### Why this is the best-supported option now

It connects the desired South Indian preference to a precise Tamil Nadu object while
avoiding unsupported claims about a whole armor system. The straight sword has a
separate South Indian catalogued precedent, but its game-scale compactness is stated
plainly as fiction. The result stays light and direct enough for fast combos; that is
a game-design proposal, not a claim about historical combat.

### Era boundary

The localized guard is dated to the **17th century**, so it is an early-modern
reference—not evidence for an ancient or medieval South Indian armor kit. The khanda
is catalogued to the 16th–17th century, while the Vijayanagara katar is second half
of the 16th century. The proposed character intentionally mixes selected visual
principles from these later surviving records with an original distant-past/future
fiction; it must not be described as a period-accurate medieval or ancient warrior.

## Deliberately excluded or deferred

- **Full historical armor, helmets, shields, rank insignia, or a named armor system:**
  unsupported by this limited evidence and likely to overwhelm a stealth/direct
  fighter. Do not invent a historical label for the single guard.
- **Royal/religious costume or temple-sculpture readings:** not used as ordinary
  martial dress evidence. They may be researched later as art-history references,
  with status/context made explicit, if the user chooses a specific polity/period.
- **Pata gauntlet sword:** a relevant-looking object is catalogued by Worcester Art
  Museum, but its current page returned an access denial during this study. It is
  therefore not evidence used for this recommendation; moreover, its integrated
  gauntlet conflicts with the requested compact one-handed sword.
- **Exact regional/national claim:** “South Indian” spans distinct places and periods.
  The localized armor cue here is Thanjavur/Tamil Nadu (17th century); the khanda is
  catalogued only as South India (16th–17th century); Vijayanagara and broad South
  India should not be silently collapsed into “Tamil.”

## Status, limits, and smallest next decision

**Supported now:** a Thanjavur/Tamil-region asymmetric arm-guard cue; South Indian
straight-blade precedent; material and date facts in the cited records.

**Not supported / unresolved:** a historically accurate ordinary warrior outfit;
the user's preferred Tamil vs. broader southern/Deccan emphasis; skin tone, hair
shape, cloth colors, exact sword length/carry, precise body ratios and stance,
rendering method, and any actual character image or gameplay animation. The broad
proportion/presentation direction is now confirmed as a lean, athletic, slightly
stylized adult in side view; it does not settle those exact ratios, the stance, or
the game camera's scale/framing.

**Source/version limits:** these are live museum catalog and API records, not versioned
software specifications. The text and image availability were checked on 1 November
2026. The Met API metadata reported a 20 December 2025 data date for the arm-guard,
Vijayanagara-katar, and chilanum records; catalog attributions/dates can be revised
by the custodian. Recheck the linked records before making a later historical or
licensing claim. No tool/service version, art-generation run, native source, export,
or Godot playback applies to this research note.

**Smallest next human decision:** choose whether to develop the recommended
*Thanjavur guard + compact straight-sword fiction* or instead research a different
specific regional/period anchor before concept comparison. If chosen, next define
the amount of cloth coverage and the sword's carry position; do not treat this note
as visual approval.
