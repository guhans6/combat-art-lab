# Character-design interview tree

Use the installed /grill-me or /grill-with-docs interview behavior. The human answers creative decisions. The agent supplies researched visual options, recommends a choice with reasons, and asks the whole current frontier in a round; do not ask dependent questions before prerequisites are resolved. Word questions so "yes" accepts the stated recommendation. Keep open/unknown answers explicit.

## Decision tree

### Round A — purpose and constraints (independent frontier)

- **Game role:** playable fighter, enemy, NPC, companion, other? What is the character supposed to communicate immediately in gameplay? If not known, recommend designing a playable single-action prototype first, as a *proposal*, not a decision.
- **Personality/fantasy:** three defining traits or an intentional contrast (e.g. relaxed outside combat, unexpectedly ferocious while striking). Do not invent clothing or gender as a default.
- **Combat/animation demands:** intended attacks, held objects, physicality, degree of exaggeration, expression importance, readable limbs and hit/contact silhouettes.
- **Project camera and target appearance:** side-view, three-quarter, top-down or not yet chosen; approximate displayed on-screen height if known. Distinguish game-view pixels from source/generated image dimensions.
- **Boundaries:** what must NOT be similar to past character or a named game? What can image generation do? Budget, transparency, provenance, exact delivery expectations.

### Round B — style references (depends on A)

Provide named game/artist candidates with actual links and images when possible. Ask which specific properties the human wants from each. Inspect the examples rather than deducing them from game titles.

Compare *proportion families* separately from *rendering families*:
- Proportions: compact/cartoon, medium stylized, lean/action-oriented, more natural/anatomical, deliberate hybrids.
- Rendering: crisp clustered pixel art, detailed pixel illustration, flatter graphical sprite, illustrated/cutout, or another route. Aseprite availability doesn't choose this.
- Motion influence: anticipation, contact, overshoot, readability and personality. Motion choice is not automatically rendered by a static image.

Ask the human which family/traits to explore, not which famous character to copy.

### Round C — visual building blocks (depends on B)

Ask about head/body ratio, silhouette masses, posture, anatomy, clothing volume, face/hair, props, palette mood, contour and shadow treatment. Present small concrete contrasts and recommend the least complicated test that can expose the intended style. Preserve role and personality.

### Round D — comparison design (depends on C)

Confirm a baseline view, comparable full-body height/framing, a stable canvas for the board, named variant axes and count, background/lighting controls, accepted image-generation method, and the review budget. Confirm whether to start with silhouettes, rendered style studies, or color studies. Only then generate the bounded board.

### Round E — selection and animation feasibility (depends on actual candidate evidence)

Ask what stays, changes, or is rejected; freeze exactly the chosen canonical image and its defining rules. Then ask which essential two or three key poses or expression states are needed to test whether the identity is practical to animate. Do not plan every frame or force a grid before the first feasible static/native test.

## Format

Use clearly numbered questions with a short recommended YES answer under each. Distinguish actual user decisions from agent recommendations. After every round, re-evaluate which branches of the tree are unblocked. End the interview only when the necessary decisions are settled and the human confirms the written shared understanding. Do not proceed from a guessed or silently inferred brief.
